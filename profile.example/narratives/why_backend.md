# Why backend and platform work

I like the problems where the answer is a number rather than an opinion.

Most of what I have enjoyed most has the same shape: something is slow, or
expensive, or wakes people up at night, and nobody is quite sure why. The work
is finding out — reading the traces, forming a theory, and then being willing to
throw the theory away when the measurement disagrees. The satisfying part is not
the fix. It is the moment the profile shows you that the thing you were certain
about was not the bottleneck at all.

Platform work suits me for a related reason. When you own the layer everyone
else builds on, a small improvement is multiplied by every team that depends on
it, and a careless one is too. That asymmetry keeps me honest. I would rather
spend a week making a change safe than a day making it fast.

I am drawn to teams that treat reliability as an engineering problem with a
budget rather than a virtue to be exhorted. Error budgets, blameless
postmortems, and an on-call rotation people can sustain are not process for its
own sake — they are how you find out what is actually breaking.
