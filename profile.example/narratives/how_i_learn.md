# How I learn

Badly, at first, and then by building something that has to work.

Reading documentation gives me the vocabulary but never the judgement. What
actually sticks is a project small enough to finish and real enough to be
annoying — where I hit the case the tutorial skipped and have to go find out why
the abstraction leaks there. Both of my side projects started as "I want to
understand this properly" and turned into things I use.

I have learned to be suspicious of the point where something starts feeling
easy. That is usually where I have built a model that happens to be right about
the cases I have tried, and the interesting failures are the ones just outside
it. So I try to look for the disagreement early: run the thing at a scale I have
not run it at, or ask someone who has operated it in production what they wish
they had known.

The other habit worth naming is writing things down before I am sure. A design
note I would be embarrassed to show anyone is usually the fastest way to find
out that I do not understand the problem yet.
